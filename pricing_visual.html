Why P* is not the price you charge. P* = e(C − E)/(e − 1) answers "what maximises this month's margin given the customers I already have". You do not have them. §05 shows what happens when demand responds to price — the two answers differ by about ₹1,900.

04 · the central trade-off

Price vs profit

Two curves. The solid one is what most models show you. The dashed one is what actually happens, because customers respond to price.

The resolution. The dashed curve is still falling at ₹3,600, the lowest price tested — so the steady-state optimum is somewhere at or below ₹3,599. But that optimum assumes you already hold 477 customers. You reach 200 in ~27 months and 477 in ~37. A price cut does not make acquisition 40% faster, so the low-price optimum is unreachable in the time that matters. Launch at ₹5,999; hold ₹4,100 as the discount floor.

06 · the binding constraint

Margin vs customer count

35% net is not a pricing problem. It is a customer-count problem — pricing only changes how many customers you need.

08 · which document do you ship

Decision tree

One question decides it. Everything else is noise.

Ship profit.md when

Margin is the metric

You have traction and need the business to stand on its own. 171 customers to 35%.

Ship price.md when

Logos are the metric

You are raising and need growth rate plus case studies. 281 customers to 35%.

Why §07 shows 183 and not 281. That chart varies the Growth price only, holding Business and Enterprise at the profit.md values. The full price.md ladder — every tier cheaper — needs 281 customers for the same margin. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions.

09 · where this breaks

Guardrails

Profit-seeking has a failure mode in a referral market. These are the moves that look accretive on a spreadsheet and cost more than they earn.

TemptationWhy it backfires
Growth at ₹9,999 Needs 135 customers instead of 171, but takes roughly 10 months longer to find them. Total profit falls.
Marking up Meta messages The one credible differentiator against AiSensy (~24%) and Interakt (~10%). Losing it for 5% margin is a bad trade.
Zero message grant on Starter Kills activation. The grant is marketing spend, not COGS. ₹100 buys a first campaign.
Gating template pre-flight Turns a pricing decision into an outage. Free users blast bad templates and you own the support ticket.
Annual discount past 20% You are buying cash with margin. Past 20% you are financing churn, not preventing it.
Pricing for 60% net At 500 customers the model says 60%. Spend it on growth instead — 35% at 3× growth beats 60% at 1×.
